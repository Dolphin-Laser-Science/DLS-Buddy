DLS-SLS-Pairs -- sample parameters
==================================

Twenty-one samples, each measured by DLS and SLS on a Brookhaven BI-200SM:
532 nm, VU geometry. DLS is eight angles, 30 to 135 deg. SLS covers the same eight
except sample 21, which is twelve angles from 30 to 140 deg.

These are quick characterisation runs, not publication-quality measurements.
The numbers below are good enough to exercise an analysis pipeline and to check
it against a known particle size; they are not reference data.

So an odd result from this set is not, by itself, a sign that the analysis is
wrong -- the measurement is the likelier suspect. It is still worth noticing: a
pattern of odd results, or two methods disagreeing about the same sample, is a
reason to look further. Use the set to see which flags fire and whether a
refusal is honest; treat a fitted value as an observation, not as a reference.


Files, per sample folder
------------------------
  DLS - NNN deg.dat          correlogram at that angle ("(rep N)" = a repeat run;
                             sample 21 carries three runs at every angle)
  Trace - NNN deg.csv        count-rate trace at 30, 90 and 135 deg (sample 12 has
                             90 deg only)
  SLS.csv                    angular scan, DERIVED -- see below
  raw/SLS - as exported.csv  the instrument's own SLS export, unmodified

The SLS files
-------------

Each SLS.csv is assembled as:

    Sample Intensity - Angle N   unchanged from raw/
    Liquid Intensity - Angle N   (I_blank - dark_blank) x f + dark_sample
                                 f = (%T / 100) x (pinhole / 0.2 mm)^2

Solvent blanks are in Solvent blanks/raw/, named with the conditions they were
measured at. Most ran at 0.2 mm and 100 %T, so f uses the sample's own values.
Two did not: the 1-propanol blank and the second water+5mM NaCl blank were taken
at their sample's own pinhole and %T, so for those f is 1 and nothing is scaled.


Data quality
------------

Angles to treat with care:

  30 deg   outlier in the gold/Pt series (13, 14, 15, 16, 19), -3 to -11 % below the
           45-90 deg trend in the raw sample data. Also outlying on 07.
  135 deg  reads +4.8 % high on the isotropic solvent blanks, and 1.2-8x above a Mie
           fit on the large-particle samples.
  45 deg   outlier on 07 only.
  75 deg   outlier on 20 only.

Samples whose count rate fluctuates, so a single 0.5 s SLS reading sits 15-30 % below
the run average: 08, 09, 10, 12, 20.

Sample 05's blank was measured at room temperature but the sample ran at 60 C

Cumulant Rh for 18 and 20 depends strongly on the fit cutoff (20: 869 -> 483 nm,
18: PDI 0.52 -> 1.13 across ordinary choices).
